Heavy Metal Pollution Index (HPI)
HPI = Σ(Wᵢ × Qᵢ) / Σ(Wᵢ)  |  Wᵢ = 1/Sᵢ (inverse of standard)  |  Qᵢ = 100 × (Mᵢ − Iᵢ) / (Sᵢ − Iᵢ)
Nemerow Pollution Index (NPI)
NPI = √[(P̄²+ P²max) / 2]  |  Pᵢ = Cᵢ / Sᵢ  (single-factor index)
Tip: Leave any metal blank to exclude it from the calculation. Values in mg/L. Standard limits from WHO / BIS IS 10500.
Results
Geoaccumulation Index (Igeo) = log₂ [ Cₙ / (1.5 × Bₙ) ]
Contamination Factor (CF) = Cₙ / Bₙ
Pollution Load Index (PLI) = (CF₁ × CF₂ × … × CFₙ)^(1/n)
Ecological Risk Factor (Eᵣ) = Tᵣ × CF  |  Risk Index (RI) = Σ Eᵣ
Tip: Values in mg/kg (dry weight). Background values (Bₙ) are pre-filled with average shale composition. Edit if you have site-specific backgrounds.

Metal Reference
Heavy Metals & Their Risks
Common heavy metals monitored in environmental samples and their health/ecological significance.

⚫ Lead (Pb)

Highly toxic neurotoxin. Bioaccumulates. WHO limit 0.01 mg/L in water. Affects brain development in children.

🟡 Cadmium (Cd)

Carcinogenic. Accumulates in kidneys. WHO limit 0.003 mg/L. Sources: fertilizers, batteries, smelting.

🔴 Arsenic (As)

Group 1 carcinogen. WHO limit 0.01 mg/L. Causes skin lesions, cancer. Naturally high in groundwater in many regions.

⚪ Mercury (Hg)

Potent neurotoxin. WHO limit 0.001 mg/L. Biomagnifies through food chain. Methylmercury is most toxic form.

🔵 Chromium (Cr)

Cr(VI) is carcinogenic. WHO limit 0.05 mg/L. Sources: leather tanning, electroplating, cement manufacturing.

🟤 Copper (Cu)

Essential micronutrient at low doses, toxic at high levels. WHO limit 2.0 mg/L. Sources: mining, agriculture.

🟠 Iron (Fe)

Most abundant heavy metal in earth's crust. Causes organoleptic issues. BIS limit 0.3 mg/L. Stains fixtures.

⚗ Manganese (Mn)

Neurological effects at high doses. WHO limit 0.1 mg/L. Co-occurs with Fe in anaerobic groundwater.

🔩 Nickel (Ni)

Allergenic and potentially carcinogenic. WHO limit 0.02 mg/L. Sources: alloy manufacturing, nickel plating.

⬜ Zinc (Zn)

Essential nutrient; toxic in excess. WHO limit 3.0 mg/L in water. Sources: galvanizing, mining runoff.

About This Project
SIH 2025 – Statement 25067

This application addresses the Smart India Hackathon 2025 problem statement on designing and developing a tool for computing Heavy Metal Pollution Indices for environmental assessment.

Heavy metal pollution poses serious threats to human health and ecosystems. Indices like HPI, Igeo, and RI provide standardised, quantifiable measures used by researchers, regulatory bodies, and environmental agencies worldwide.

Indices Implemented
HPI Heavy Metal Pollution Index (Mohan et al., 1996)
NPI Nemerow Pollution Index (Nemerow, 1974)
Igeo Geoaccumulation Index (Müller, 1969)
CF/PLI Contamination Factor & Pollution Load Index (Tomlinson, 1980)
RI/Eᵣ Ecological Risk Index (Hakanson, 1980)
